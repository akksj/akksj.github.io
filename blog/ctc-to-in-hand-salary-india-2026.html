CTC to In-Hand Salary in India (FY 2026-27): How to Calculate It, With Worked Examples

Updated 16 October 2026 · FY 2026-27 rules

Your offer letter says ₹20 LPA. Your first salary credit says something closer to ₹1.2 lakh. Nobody lied to you: CTC (cost to company) is what the employer spends on you in a year, and only part of it reaches your bank account each month. This guide shows exactly where the rest goes, using FY 2026-27 new-regime tax rules, with three worked examples you can check against our free CTC to in-hand salary calculator.

The short version

In-hand salary = CTC − employer-side costs (employer PF, gratuity, insurance) − employee PF − professional tax − income tax.

Variable pay is part of CTC but usually arrives once or twice a year, so it shouldn't be counted in your monthly budget.

Step 1: Take out what never reaches your account

Most Indian tech CTCs include a few items that the company pays on your behalf:

  • Employer PF contribution. Typically 12% of basic salary, deposited into your EPF account. It's your money, but you can't spend it monthly. Some companies calculate it on a capped basic of ₹15,000/month (₹1,800/month PF) instead of full basic. Check your offer.
  • Gratuity. Often shown as about 4.81% of basic (15 days' basic for every year of service). It's paid only when you leave after at least five years of continuous service (with limited exceptions). If you switch every two to three years, you may never receive it.
  • Insurance premiums and other employer costs. Some offers include group health insurance premiums in CTC. Treat these as benefits, not salary.

What's left is your gross salary: the number that appears at the top of your payslip and that tax is calculated on.

Step 2: Work out taxable income

Under the new tax regime, salaried people get a flat ₹75,000 standard deduction. Most other deductions (80C, HRA, home-loan interest on a self-occupied house) aren't available in the new regime, so for most developers:

Taxable income = gross salary − ₹75,000

Step 3: Apply the FY 2026-27 new-regime slabs

Budget 2026 left the new-regime slabs unchanged from FY 2025-26:

Taxable incomeRate
Up to ₹4 lakhNil
₹4–8 lakh5%
₹8–12 lakh10%
₹12–16 lakh15%
₹16–20 lakh20%
₹20–24 lakh25%
Above ₹24 lakh30%

Two adjustments matter a lot at developer salaries:

  • Rebate up to ₹12 lakh. If taxable income is ₹12 lakh or less, a rebate of up to ₹60,000 wipes out the tax completely. This was section 87A of the Income-tax Act, 1961, and is section 156 of the Income-tax Act, 2025, which applies from 1 April 2026. With the standard deduction, a gross salary up to ₹12.75 lakh pays no income tax.
  • Marginal relief just above ₹12 lakh. Without it, earning ₹12,10,000 instead of ₹12,00,000 would cost ₹61,500 in tax. Marginal relief caps your tax at the amount above ₹12 lakh, so the tax is ₹10,000 (plus cess). The relief fades out at roughly ₹12.7 lakh of taxable income.

Finally, add the 4% health and education cess on the tax. Surcharge applies only above ₹50 lakh of taxable income, which isn't relevant for most people reading this.

Step 4: Subtract employee PF and professional tax

  • Employee PF: 12% of basic (or of the capped ₹15,000 basic), deducted from your salary every month.
  • Professional tax: a state tax, typically around ₹2,400–2,500 a year, deducted monthly in states that levy it.

Worked examples (FY 2026-27, new regime)

All three assume basic is 40% of CTC, and that employer PF (12% of full basic) and gratuity (4.81% of basic) are inside the CTC, with ₹2,400 professional tax. Figures are rounded to the nearest rupee.

About 88% of CTC reaches your account, and you pay no income tax. Open this example in the calculator.

Example 2: ₹15 lakh CTC, no variable

Gross salary is ₹13,99,140 and taxable income ₹13,24,140. That's above ₹12 lakh and past the marginal-relief zone, so full slab tax applies: ₹78,621 plus ₹3,145 cess, or ₹81,766 in total. After ₹72,000 employee PF and ₹2,400 professional tax, in-hand is ₹12,42,974 a year, about ₹1,03,581 a month.

A useful threshold: about ₹13.66 lakh CTC

With 40% basic and PF and gratuity inside CTC, a CTC of up to about ₹13.66 lakh works out to a gross salary of ₹12.75 lakh or less, which means zero income tax. If you're negotiating around this range, small changes in CTC structure can move your tax noticeably. Run your actual breakup rather than relying on this rule of thumb.

What to check in your offer letter

  1. Fixed vs variable. Is the variable "up to" a target? What was the payout last year? Ask the recruiter.
  2. Basic percentage. A higher basic means more PF (less monthly cash, more savings) and a larger gratuity provision.
  3. PF on full or capped basic. This can change monthly in-hand by several thousand rupees.
  4. One-time items. Joining bonuses, relocation and retention bonuses are sometimes folded into "first-year CTC". Strip them out to compare like with like.
  5. ESOPs/RSUs. Treat them as upside, not salary. Vesting schedules, cliffs and taxation vary a lot.

Limitations

This method fits a resident salaried employee on the new regime with no other income. It doesn't cover HRA or other old-regime exemptions, employer NPS, meal cards, mid-year joining (TDS is spread over fewer months), or ESOP taxation. Your employer's payroll may round or sequence things differently. Use the result to compare offers, and check your first payslip.
